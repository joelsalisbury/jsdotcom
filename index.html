I design and build digital things, lead teams that do the same, and spend a lot of time figuring out how technology, people, institutions, and good ideas can work together a little better.

The part I enjoy most is doing that work with people: building trust, forming relationships, getting the right folks in the room, and helping a group turn a fuzzy problem into something useful.

These days

I lead Institutional Insights & Innovation (i3) at the University of Connecticut. Our work sits somewhere between product development, design, software, data, institutional strategy, research, and whatever problem needs solving next.

We build and support digital products and services for the university, work alongside partners on research and institutional projects, and experiment with new ways of using technology to make complicated things simpler, more useful, and more humane.

A lot of my job is less about having the answer myself and more about helping people find it together. I like connecting people who should know each other, translating across disciplines, and building the relationships that make ambitious work possible.

Before that

I've spent most of my career making things for the web and helping other people make better things for the web.

My work has moved across design, UX, product, software development, accessibility, digital strategy, and creative technology. I previously led UConn's Digital Experience Group, and I still like getting close to the work: sketching interfaces, prototyping ideas, writing Laravel, arguing with architecture diagrams, and occasionally remembering that I am supposed to be in management.

When they'll still have me, I also enjoy teaching as an adjunct instructor with UConn's Digital Media & Design department.

What I'm interested in

Good products. Useful tech. Small teams with a lot of trust. Accessible design. Sensible technology governance. Public-interest software. Laravel. AI that actually helps people. And work that happens through collaboration rather than around it.

I'm especially interested in the human side of technical work: how relationships, trust, curiosity, and a willingness to talk to each other can turn complicated organizational problems into things people can actually understand and use.
